# Rider brake-intent interface — partial topology milestone

**NOT SAFE TO ENERGIZE. All MCU firmware remains unimplemented.** This is a candidate electrical interface and executable policy model, not a working brake controller, safety certification, sensor installation approval or PCB release. Mechanical brakes must remain independently adequate; electric braking is supplemental. No cable alteration, inline load-bearing sensor, purchase or PCB layout was performed.

## Delivered topology and changed safety boundary

The old `BRAKE_LOOP` combined rider braking and fault shutdown; it could not allow controlled negative torque. J5 is now `ESTOP_THERMAL_FAULT_CHAIN_NC`: pin1 SAFETY3V3, pin2 FAULT_CHAIN. Only normally-closed emergency-stop and dump overtemperature contacts belong here, **not rider lever contacts**. Open wiring pulls FAULT_CHAIN down through the existing 10k. U6 pin11 compares it against REF2V5; pin13 still sinks ANALOG_OK on fault. Auxiliary/MCU rail monitors, raw fast OC, watchdog, hardware enable gating, timer-break connections and release-before-rearm latch are retained. PD14/pin61 now observes FAULT_CHAIN_SENSE. This is an incompatible harness change; never silently reuse the old harness.

Normal braking instead reaches four ADC inputs. Healthy firmware must prohibit **all positive hub torque** whenever either lever requests braking, while retaining controlled negative hub torque if an independently permitted energy sink exists. Merely allowing bridge ENABLE does not enforce torque sign. **There is NO hardware-independent positive-torque guard in this milestone.** MCU_ARM is not proof of torque sign. The prior blanket brake kill has deliberately been separated, not replaced by an equivalent independent torque guard. A controller that feeds the watchdog while issuing wrong-sign torque remains dangerous. G0/G9 release remain blocked until independent torque-sign supervision/current-and-rotor-direction checking or a comparably justified architecture is implemented and tested. Do not interpret these edits as permission to energize.

Normal resistor braking has an explicit path: PE0/pin50 `BRAKE_DUMP_PWM` → U25 AND with TIMER_BREAK_OK → U24 OR with OV_CMD → U10 pin6. A pulldown makes PWM default low. Hardware watchdog, E-stop, OC or thermal kill suppresses normal dump PWM as well as bridges. OV_CMD bypasses this gate deliberately: emergency bus clamping is independent of rider request and may cause unavoidable drag through PM diode rectification. Gate-off is not generation-off. OV with a hot dump, lost AUX12V or failed resistor is still unresolved; no safe thermal guarantee. A malicious/failed MCU that continues valid watchdog transitions can still dump energy without request; software contract is not independent authorization.

## Actual interface components

New native pages: `brake-intent-left.kicad_sch`, `brake-intent-right.kicad_sch`, `brake-switch-harness.kicad_sch`, `brake-dump-command.kicad_sch`. J44 left/J45 right each have pin1 locally series-fed 3V3, pin2 PGND, pin3 Hall voltage, pin4 supervised switch. Connector family, sealing, strain relief and physical keying remain unselected (empty footprint is intentional, not fab ready).

Each sensor feed has 22Ω series resistance and 100nF board-side decoupling. This limits fault current but is **not** a sustained-short-safe supply switch: a hard short can dissipate about 0.50W in the resistor and disturb the MCU rail; add qualified per-cable current limiting before release. DRV5055 maximum 10mA would lose 0.22V; a guaranteed upstream minimum above 3.22V plus cable drop is needed to maintain its 3.0V operating floor. External rail tolerance/current budget and remote 100nF at the actual Hall sensor remain integration requirements. Hall ICs are candidates, not fictitiously fitted board components.

Each of four signals has PESD3V3U1UL to ground at the connector, 10k series into ADC, 10nF to ground at ADC, and BAT54S rail clamps. Hall lines have 100k pulldowns so disconnected signal tends to zero; binary lines have 10k pullups. The nominal series RC is 100µs, but source impedance increases switch settling; firmware acquisition/filter latency is unqualified. BAT54S injection into LOGIC3V3_EXT requires rail sink/backpower validation; its forward voltage is not a demonstrated guarantee of STM32 absolute maximum protection. TVS 3.3V standoff is not its transient clamp voltage. No battery-voltage miswire, ESD/EMC or power-off withstand claim.

Offboard EOL network per lever: 4.7k series from signal to `(10k || NC lever switch)` to ground. Resistors must be at the lever so cable disconnection removes both. Released contact closed gives 1.055V nominal (0.320 of rail); braking opens it, giving 1.964V (0.595 of rail). Whole cable open gives rail; short gives ground. Proposed ratio windows: release 0.27–0.38, brake 0.53–0.66; all other values invalid. These are engineering starting windows, not qualified thresholds. Detect loss within a bounded diagnostic interval, latch electronic torque off, and require repaired sensors plus released levers and explicit reset. A welded switch/EOL resistor short can mimic release; compare independently with Hall travel. Common ground faults, cross-shorts and simultaneous plausible stuck sensors are not fully covered. The two lever channels are independent request sources, not two identical redundant measurements of one lever.

Hall initial validity envelope proposed 0.10–0.90 of the measured supply; open/short outside is fault. Calibrate each lever's release and full-travel values and polarity, subtract a measured release deadband, check switch/Hall agreement with bounded debounce, monotonicity, supply plausibility and stale samples. Do not enable solely because a raw voltage is in range. Magnet absent may yield plausible midpoint, so calibration and redundant mechanical switch comparison are mandatory; startup proof and persistent stuck detection are unresolved. No final magnet gap, fullscale voltage or sensitivity suffix is selected.

At 3.3V, A1/A2/A3/A4 nominal 60/30/15/7.5mV/mT, ±22/44/88/176mT linear ranges, midpoint VCC/2. Headline 5V sensitivities do not apply. DBZ SOT23 pins1 VCC,2 OUT,3 GND; LPG TO92 pins1 VCC,2 GND,3 OUT. South pole facing marked package side raises output. Candidate architecture only; no chosen suffix or package.
- [TI DRV5053](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv5053.pdf): alternative 2.5–38V supply, approximately 1V zero-field and 0.2–1.8V signal, not ratiometric midpoint. PA is −23mV/mT, CA +23mV/mT. Same DBZ/LPG mappings, but polarity depends suffix. It requires different calibration/windows; not a drop-in firmware assumption.
- [Nexperia BAT54S](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/BAT54S.pdf) §5: pin1 A1→PGND, pin2 K2→LOGIC3V3_EXT, pin3 K1/A2→ADC. Not BAT54A/C. [PESD3V3U1UL](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PESD3V3U1UL.pdf) §5: pin1 K→external signal, pin2 A→ground, SOD882.
- [TI SN74LVC1G32](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g32.pdf) and [SN74LVC1G08](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g08.pdf): DBV pins1 A,2 B,3 GND,4 Y,5 VCC. Powered from SAFETY3V3; no claim that logic gates prove torque sign.
- ST DS12288 Rev5 Table12, recovered primary-authored PDF and provenance in `hardware/powerboard-reva/docs/mcu-source-evidence.md`: PC3 pin18 ADC12_IN9 (left Hall); PA3 pin25 ADC1_IN4 (right Hall); PA4 pin26 ADC2_IN17 (left switch); PB2 pin34 ADC2_IN12 (right switch); PE0 pin50 GPIO (dump PWM). PA4 DAC must remain disabled; all four are analog inputs. ADC scheduling with existing phase channels remains unimplemented. Direct current ST fetch failed (HTTP2 error, HTTP1 timeout); recovered ST source used instead, current revision/errata validation still required.

Reversible, non-load-bearing sensor mounts on both levers are the recommendation, not approved exact parts. Magnet size/grade/orientation/gap, lever geometry, nearby steel, alignment drift, temperature and usable travel must be measured. Linear field sensing does not imply linear lever gap response.

## Selected sensing direction: FSR pressure pad at brake lever — next milestone

**User clarification supersedes the earlier ambiguous bridge request: an FSR force-sensitive resistor/pressure pad at the lever is intended, NOT metallic DMS/strain gauge.** The fitted candidate Hall interface delivered here is now an alternative/reference, not the selected rider sensor. Next bounded task is FSR sourcing and sensor-specific divider/conductance conditioning plus calibration; no exact MPN, force range or mounting geometry is selected. Keep an independent binary mechanical brake activation channel. Zero/released FSR pressure must NEVER override an active binary brake: it must inhibit positive torque and treat disagreement as fault, not interpret it as release. FSR hysteresis, creep, nonlinearity, preload, aging and temperature require measured calibration and diagnostic thresholds. A passive R-2R network does not generally linearize the FSR. No unbounded front-end rewrite is included here; the existing Hall-to-switch agreement model remains a model of the alternative, not validated FSR decoding.

### Earlier bridge proposal — not selected / not fitted

The earlier Wheatstone bridge request was ambiguous and is not a selected circuit. A future actual metallic bridge would need excitation, instrumentation/PGA and ratiometric ADC checks; these are **not** an FSR implementation and are not fitted or reserved as the selected AFE. R-2R may serve a buffered calibration-injection/offset/reference function if justified later, disconnected from safety thresholds, but not automatic sensor linearization. No fixed resistor/gain values are invented without the selected FSR's resistance-versus-force range and headroom budget. No inline hydraulic/cable or load-bearing experiment is authorized.

## Controller contract and model limits

`analysis/brake_intent/model.py` is an executable **normalized state model, NOT MCU firmware**. Sensor decoding, debounce, diagnostics, ADC/PWM configuration, motor commutation/current control, rotor direction, torque limits, thermal sensing, BMS communications and rearm registers are all unimplemented. Input `battery_accepts` represents independently validated charge permission/current limit, not inference from brake request; no physical BMS permission interface was added. `dump_available_cool` represents a yet-to-be-implemented conservative temperature/capacity limit. Unknown permissions must deny that sink.

No request: commanded regen and dump both zero even if battery accepts. Brake with charge acceptance: negative torque into battery. Full pack: negative torque only if cool qualified dump available. Full pack plus hot/unavailable dump: electronic torque zero, mechanical brakes required. Sensor disagreement/disconnect: electronic torque zero and latched disarm via firmware; E-stop/OC/watchdog: hardware inhibit regardless of firmware. Watchdog timeout is not instantaneous; moving torque during detection latency requires a bounded validation budget. The model's reset boolean is an abstract event, not replacement for the existing physical release-before-rearm memory.

The emergency OV clamp may activate with no brake request, including gates off. That exception is bus protection, not rider-requested braking. PM backfeed can cause drag even without an OV event; the model does not simulate that electromechanical behavior. Thermal availability cannot make an unsafe descent safe; retain mechanical braking capacity and account for total bike+rider+luggage mass.
